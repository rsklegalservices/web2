Frequently Asked Questions

What is service law? +

Service law generally concerns the legal rights, duties and conditions of employment applicable to employees, particularly government and public-sector employees governed by specific service rules and regulations.

Can a government employee challenge a termination order? +

Depending on the applicable service rules and legal framework, a government employee may have remedies to challenge termination, dismissal or removal from service.

Can a suspension order be challenged? +

A suspension order may be challengeable in appropriate circumstances, depending on the applicable service rules, facts and available legal remedies.

Can an employee challenge a transfer order? +

A transfer may be challenged in limited circumstances where a legally recognised ground exists. The availability of a remedy depends on the applicable service rules, transfer policy and facts of the case.

Can an employee challenge denial of promotion? +

Depending on the applicable service rules and circumstances, an employee may have remedies concerning promotion, seniority or eligibility disputes.

Can pension be claimed after retirement? +

Pension and retirement benefits depend on the applicable employment rules, service period and eligibility requirements. Disputes regarding entitlement or release of benefits may be pursued through appropriate legal remedies.

Can a private employee challenge termination? +

A private employee may have contractual, statutory or other legal remedies depending on the nature of employment, applicable law and circumstances surrounding termination.

What should I do if I receive a charge memo? +

A charge memo should be reviewed carefully against the applicable service rules and facts. An employee may need to submit an appropriate response within the prescribed time.

Can service matters be taken to the High Court? +

Certain service matters may be brought before the High Court depending on jurisdiction, the employee's status and the availability of other statutory or tribunal remedies.
