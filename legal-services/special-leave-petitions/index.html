Special Leave Petition Lawyers

A Special Leave Petition is a constitutional remedy under Article 136 of the Constitution of India through which a party may seek special leave to appeal against a judgment, decree, determination, sentence or order passed by a court or tribunal.

Our advocates assist clients in evaluating the legal issues involved and preparing appropriate proceedings before the Supreme Court, depending on the facts and circumstances of the case.

SLP Under Article 136

Article 136 confers a discretionary power on the Supreme Court to grant special leave to appeal against specified judgments, decrees, determinations, sentences or orders.

An SLP is not an ordinary appeal as of right. The petition must set out appropriate legal grounds and comply with the applicable procedural requirements of the Supreme Court.

Grounds for Special Leave Petition

The grounds raised in an SLP depend upon the facts and legal issues involved in the particular case.

Potential grounds may concern questions of law, jurisdictional issues, procedural irregularities, interpretation of statutory provisions, constitutional issues or other legal errors that may warrant consideration by the Supreme Court.

The appropriate grounds must be determined after reviewing the complete record and impugned judgment or order.

SLP Admission & Preliminary Proceedings

After filing, the matter may be considered by the Supreme Court at the appropriate stage. The Court may examine whether the matter warrants the exercise of its jurisdiction under Article 136.

Our advocates assist clients with the preparation and presentation of the case at the relevant stage of the proceedings.

Interim Relief & Stay in SLP Matters

In appropriate cases, parties may seek interim relief or stay of the operation or implementation of the impugned judgment or order.

Applications for interim relief must be supported by appropriate facts and legal grounds, and the grant of relief remains subject to the Court's consideration.

SLP Conversion into Appeal

Where the Supreme Court grants special leave, the matter proceeds in accordance with the applicable appellate procedure.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a Special Leave Petition (SLP)? +

A Special Leave Petition is a petition filed before the Supreme Court under Article 136 of the Constitution seeking special permission to appeal against an eligible judgment, decree, determination, sentence or order.

Can an SLP be filed against a High Court judgment? +

An SLP may be filed before the Supreme Court against an eligible High Court judgment or order, subject to the requirements of Article 136 and applicable Supreme Court procedure.

Is an SLP an appeal as of right? +

No. The jurisdiction under Article 136 is discretionary. The Supreme Court decides whether special leave should be granted in the particular matter.

Can an SLP be filed against a tribunal order? +

In appropriate cases, an SLP may be considered against a tribunal or other eligible authority's decision. The specific remedy depends on the nature of the order and applicable law.

Can an SLP be filed in criminal cases? +

Yes, appropriate criminal matters may be brought before the Supreme Court through an SLP, subject to the applicable legal and procedural requirements.

Can I seek a stay while filing an SLP? +

A party may seek interim relief or stay in appropriate circumstances. Whether such relief is granted depends on the facts and the Supreme Court's consideration of the application.

What documents are required for an SLP? +

The documents required depend on the case. They may include the impugned judgment or order, relevant pleadings, previous orders, supporting documents and other records necessary for presenting the matter before the Supreme Court.

How can PR Sudheer Kumar & Co assist with an SLP? +

Our advocates can assist with reviewing the impugned judgment or order, identifying legal issues, drafting the SLP and connected applications, preparing supporting documents and assisting with proceedings before the Supreme Court, subject to the requirements of the case.
