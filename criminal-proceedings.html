Anticipatory Bail

Frequently Asked Questions

What is anticipatory bail? +

Anticipatory bail is a legal remedy that may be available to a person who has reason to apprehend arrest in connection with an alleged offence, subject to the applicable law and court's consideration.

What is regular bail? +

Regular bail is bail sought after a person has been arrested or is otherwise in custody, subject to the applicable procedural law and jurisdiction of the concerned court.

Can an FIR be quashed? +

In appropriate circumstances, a person may approach the competent High Court seeking quashing of an FIR or related criminal proceedings. Whether such a petition is maintainable depends on the facts, allegations and applicable law.

Can criminal proceedings be quashed after investigation? +

The availability of a quashing remedy after investigation depends on the circumstances of the case, the allegations, the case records and applicable legal principles. Legal advice should be obtained after reviewing the relevant documents.

What is a criminal revision? +

A criminal revision is a specific legal remedy available in circumstances prescribed by law to challenge certain orders of subordinate criminal courts.

Can I seek bail before the High Court? +

Depending on the circumstances and applicable law, bail or anticipatory bail may be sought before the High Court. The appropriate forum depends on the nature and stage of the proceedings.

Can a person challenge an FIR without waiting for the trial? +

In appropriate circumstances, a person may seek judicial remedies concerning an FIR or criminal proceedings before the trial concludes. The availability of such a remedy depends on the facts and applicable law.

What should I do if I receive a police notice? +

A police notice should not be ignored. The appropriate response depends on the nature of the notice, the allegations and the applicable procedural law. Obtaining legal advice promptly can help determine the appropriate course of action.

Can criminal cases be appealed? +

Certain criminal judgments, convictions, sentences and orders may be appealable under applicable law. The availability and forum for an appeal depend on the nature of the case and the order being challenged.
