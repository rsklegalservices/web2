Section 138 Cheque Bounce Cases

Section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881 provides a statutory framework concerning dishonour of cheques in specified circumstances.

A cheque dishonour matter may involve several legal requirements, including presentation of the cheque, dishonour, issuance of the statutory demand notice, the prescribed period for payment and initiation of proceedings within the applicable limitation period.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a cheque bounce case? +

A cheque bounce case generally refers to legal proceedings arising from dishonour of a cheque in circumstances covered by applicable law, including Section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881.

What happens when a cheque is dishonoured? +

The legal consequences depend on the reason for dishonour, the underlying transaction and the requirements of the applicable law. In matters falling within Section 138, specific statutory steps and timelines may apply.

Is a legal notice required in a cheque bounce case? +

For proceedings under Section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act, the statutory requirements concerning notice must be considered carefully. The timing and contents of the notice are important and should be assessed based on the facts and applicable law.

Can I send a cheque bounce notice? +

A person legally entitled to pursue the claim may issue an appropriate statutory notice where the requirements of the applicable law are satisfied. The notice should be prepared after reviewing the cheque, bank return memo and underlying transaction.

What should I do if I receive a cheque bounce notice? +

The notice should be reviewed promptly. The underlying transaction, cheque, bank records, allegations and applicable statutory requirements should be examined before deciding whether to reply, make payment, negotiate a settlement or pursue another legally available remedy.

Can a security cheque lead to a cheque bounce case? +

A dispute involving a security cheque requires examination of the underlying transaction and legally enforceable liability, along with the other statutory requirements. The legal position depends on the facts and applicable law.

Can a cheque bounce case be settled? +

Depending on the circumstances and applicable law, parties may explore settlement or compounding of a cheque dishonour matter. The procedure can depend on the stage of the proceedings and the applicable legal provisions.

Can a cheque bounce case be appealed? +

Certain judgments and orders arising from cheque bounce proceedings may be subject to appeal, revision or other remedies where permitted by law. The appropriate remedy depends on the nature of the order and stage of the case.

How long does a cheque bounce case take? +

The duration varies depending on the court, procedural stages, evidence, applications, settlement possibilities and other circumstances. No fixed timeframe can be guaranteed.

Can a cheque bounce case be filed against a company? +

Where the statutory requirements are satisfied, proceedings may arise from a cheque issued in connection with a company or business transaction. The liability of the company and any individuals depends on the applicable statutory provisions and facts of the case.
