Frequently Asked Questions

What is a tribunal? +

A tribunal is a specialized adjudicatory body established under applicable legislation to deal with particular categories of disputes and legal matters.

What matters are handled by the NCLT? +

The NCLT deals with specified company law and insolvency-related matters under the applicable corporate and insolvency framework.

What is the DRT? +

The Debt Recovery Tribunal is a specialized forum dealing with specified debt recovery matters involving banks, financial institutions and other eligible creditors under applicable law.

What matters can be taken before the CAT? +

The Central Administrative Tribunal deals with specified service-related disputes involving employees and applicants falling within its jurisdiction, including certain matters concerning recruitment, promotion, transfer, disciplinary action and retirement benefits.

Can a company be represented before the NCLT? +

Yes. Companies and other eligible parties may appear through appropriate authorized legal representation in NCLT proceedings, subject to the applicable procedural requirements.

Can borrowers approach the DRT? +

Depending on the nature of the dispute and applicable law, borrowers and other affected parties may have remedies before the DRT or other competent forums.

Can government employees approach the CAT? +

Eligible government employees and other persons falling within CAT's jurisdiction may pursue appropriate service-related remedies before the Tribunal.

Can a tribunal order be challenged? +

Depending on the governing legislation and nature of the order, an appeal, judicial review or another legal remedy may be available. The appropriate remedy must be determined on the facts and applicable law.

Can interim relief be sought before a tribunal? +

Yes. Where legally permissible, parties may seek appropriate interim relief during the pendency of tribunal proceedings.

What documents are required for tribunal proceedings? +

The required documents depend on the type of proceeding. They may include the challenged order, agreements, notices, correspondence, service records, financial documents, pleadings and other supporting materials relevant to the dispute.
