Habeas Corpus Petitions

A Habeas Corpus petition is a constitutional remedy concerning unlawful detention or deprivation of personal liberty.

Our lawyers assist with appropriate Habeas Corpus proceedings where a person is alleged to be unlawfully detained or where judicial intervention is sought concerning the legality of detention.

Such proceedings are considered based on the facts of the case and the constitutional and legal framework applicable to the matter.

Mandamus Petitions

A Mandamus petition may be sought in appropriate circumstances to require a public authority or public official to perform a legal duty.

We assist clients in matters involving alleged failure or refusal by public authorities to perform duties required by law.

Depending on the circumstances, matters may involve government departments, statutory authorities, public bodies or other authorities falling within the applicable writ jurisdiction.

Certiorari Petitions

A Certiorari remedy may be sought in appropriate cases to challenge certain orders or decisions of authorities, tribunals or bodies where grounds for judicial review exist.

Our advocates assist clients in preparing and pursuing appropriate proceedings challenging administrative or quasi-judicial decisions, subject to the jurisdiction and applicable legal requirements.

Prohibition Petitions

A Prohibition remedy may be available in appropriate circumstances where a court, tribunal or authority is alleged to be proceeding beyond its lawful jurisdiction.

We assist clients in examining the legal issues involved and pursuing appropriate constitutional remedies where legally maintainable.

Quo Warranto Petitions

A Quo Warranto proceeding may be concerned with the legal authority of a person to hold a public office.

Our advocates assist with appropriate constitutional proceedings involving questions concerning eligibility or lawful entitlement to occupy a public office, subject to applicable law and jurisdiction.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a writ petition? +

A writ petition is a constitutional legal proceeding through which a person may seek judicial remedies in appropriate circumstances, particularly concerning fundamental rights, legal rights and actions or omissions of public authorities.

What are the main types of writs? +

The principal constitutional writs include Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Certiorari, Prohibition and Quo Warranto.

Can I file a writ petition against a government order? +

A government order or administrative decision may be challenged through writ jurisdiction in appropriate circumstances. Whether a writ petition is maintainable depends on the facts, jurisdiction, legal grounds and availability of other remedies.

Can a writ petition be filed for violation of fundamental rights? +

Yes, writ jurisdiction may provide a constitutional remedy in appropriate cases involving infringement of fundamental rights. The appropriate court and remedy depend on the circumstances of the case.

What is a Mandamus petition? +

Mandamus is a constitutional remedy that may, in appropriate circumstances, be used to seek an order requiring a public authority or official to perform a legal duty.

What is Habeas Corpus? +

Habeas Corpus is a constitutional remedy concerning unlawful detention or deprivation of personal liberty. Courts examine the legality of the detention based on the facts and applicable law.

Can a writ petition be filed against an administrative authority? +

Yes, in appropriate circumstances, actions, decisions or omissions of government and public authorities may be challenged through writ jurisdiction, subject to constitutional and legal requirements.

Can a writ petition be defended? +

Yes. A government authority, public body or other respondent may need to defend a writ petition by filing an appropriate response or counter-affidavit and presenting its legal position before the court.

Is a writ petition always the first remedy? +

Not necessarily. The availability of an alternative statutory remedy can be relevant to whether a High Court should exercise writ jurisdiction. The appropriate remedy must be determined based on the facts and applicable law.
