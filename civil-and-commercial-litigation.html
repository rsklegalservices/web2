Frequently Asked Questions

What is civil litigation? +

Civil litigation involves legal proceedings concerning civil rights, property, contracts, money claims, possession, injunctions and other disputes that are generally not criminal prosecutions.

What types of property disputes can a civil lawyer handle? +

Civil property litigation may involve ownership, title, possession, partition, boundaries, agreements, injunctions, declaration and other disputes concerning property rights.

Can I file a suit to recover money owed to me? +

Depending on the transaction and available evidence, a person or business may be able to initiate appropriate recovery proceedings. Limitation, documentation and the nature of the underlying claim are important considerations.

Can I obtain an injunction in a property dispute? +

An injunction may be available in appropriate circumstances where legal requirements are satisfied. The court considers the facts, legal rights and applicable principles before granting such relief.

Can a contract dispute be taken to court? +

Yes. Depending on the agreement and applicable law, contractual disputes may be pursued or defended through appropriate civil or commercial proceedings.

What is commercial litigation? +

Commercial litigation involves disputes arising from business transactions, contracts, commercial relationships, payments, partnerships and other business-related matters.

Should I send a legal notice before filing a civil case? +

A legal notice may be appropriate in certain disputes, although whether it is necessary or advisable depends on the nature of the claim and applicable law.

How long does a civil case take? +

The duration of litigation varies significantly depending on the nature of the dispute, court, procedural stages, evidence, complexity and other circumstances. No fixed timeframe can be guaranteed.

Can a civil judgment be appealed? +

Certain judgments, decrees and orders may be appealable under applicable law. The available remedy depends on the nature of the decision and the governing procedural provisions.
